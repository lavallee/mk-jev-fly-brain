    How to play

    Fly brain is a 12,000-neuron slice of the Janelia FlyEM maleCNS v1.0 connectome, simulated spike by spike in a background thread. The fight drives real cell types: the opponent moving reaches LC9, its size reaches LC10a, lunges and strikes reach the looming detectors LPLC2 and LC4, closeness arouses male-specific P1, foreleg contact reaches the taste bristles, and hits reach the body's touch bristles. The busiest motor pool picks the move: DNp09 walks forward, MDN walks back, the giant-fiber jump neuron TTMn jumps away, front legs punch, hind legs kick, and the wing muscles raise a guard. Two reflexes come straight from the wiring: movement pulls the fly forward (LC9 to DNp09), and a looming strike launches the escape jump. Nothing we sense reliably reaches MDN, so the fly retreats by jumping, not by walking back. During a match the fly learns: dopamine signals how much better or worse an exchange went than expected: landing a hit or blocking a strike is better, eating a full-damage strike is worse. That strengthens or weakens the recently active synapses into the motor pool whose move it actually made, so reflexes that keep getting it punched fade and ones that pay off grow. Flies do this kind of learning mainly in the mushroom body; putting it on the motor inputs is our simplification. The wiring is real. The neuron physics, the senses, the move mapping and the learning rule are assumptions. Every match is saved. What the fly learned during a match is kept with it, and the Starting brain menu can start a new match from the wired connectome or from the best brain learned so far.

    Jev (TypeSafe jev-latest) gets life, moves, distance and time left as JSON about ten times a second and picks one of the same seven moves. Fly brain always fights as Sub-Zero on the left and Jev as Kano on the right. A round ends on a knockout or when the clock runs out. Game engine: mk.js by Minko Gechev (MIT). Music, blocks, whooshes, the bell and the KO boom are synthesized in your browser; punches, kicks and reactions are recorded sounds from Pixabay (credits); the announcer was voiced once with ElevenLabs.
